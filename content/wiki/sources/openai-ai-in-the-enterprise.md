## Summary

Seven lessons for enterprise AI adoption, each illustrated with a customer story. Also describes OpenAI's org split into Research, Applied and Deployment teams and its "iterative deployment" approach.

## Key claims

- Lessons: start with evals; embed AI in products; start now and invest early; customise and fine-tune; put AI in the hands of experts; unblock developers; set bold automation goals.
- Morgan Stanley ran evals for every proposed application (translation, summarisation, comparison against expert advisors) before production; 98% of advisors now use it daily and document access rose from 20% to 80%.
- Indeed's GPT-powered "why this job" explanations raised applications started by 20% and downstream success (employers more likely to hire) by 13% when tested against the previous matching engine; a fine-tuned smaller model then matched results with 60% fewer tokens.
- Klarna's assistant handled two-thirds of service chats within months, cutting resolution time from 11 to 2 minutes, with a projected $40M profit improvement and satisfaction scores on par with human support; results came from continuous testing and refinement.
- Lowe's fine-tuning improved product-tagging accuracy by 20% and error detection by 60%.
- BBVA rolled out ChatGPT Enterprise with Legal, Compliance and IT Security involved, and employees built 2,900+ custom GPTs in five months.
- Mercado Libre built an internal platform (Verdi) with security, guardrails and routing built in for 17,000 developers.
- Successful companies align on high-return, low-effort use cases, iterate, and back the work with rigorous evals and guardrails.
