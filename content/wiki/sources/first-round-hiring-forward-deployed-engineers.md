## Summary

Interviews a former Palantir FDE and FDE recruiter, the founders of Serval and Genera (ex-Ironclad "legal engineering"), and Looker's founders. Gives a diagnostic for when FDEs make sense, the value they create, the ways the function goes wrong, and hiring traits.

## Key claims

- An FDE embeds with a customer to build the final stretch that makes a product work in production; the role writes and debugs production code and is not a sales-engineer or solutions-consultant role.
- The FDE is not an implementation role: Palantir's model relied on creativity and decentralised product development, building incrementally from concrete customer use cases.
- Three-part diagnostic for hiring FDEs: an upmarket (large-enterprise) focus, a product vision willing to be shaped by customers, and heterogeneous customers with demanding problems. Product-led or freemium models fit poorly.
- Check the unit economics first: Looker only funded FDE-style pre-sales once the per-customer economics worked ("in the neighborhood of $25,000 a year per customer", per Frank Bien).
- Value: closing large deals with demos on customer data, discovering product opportunities that short discovery calls miss, removing middlemen from the feedback loop, clearing low-priority backlog items.
- Failure modes: drifting into a services/implementation shop, hiring an FDE when you need a sales or implementation role, endless bespoke work with no software upside, one-off features without product sense, sending FDEs to every customer instead of high-value accounts. Ironclad reserves legal engineers for high-ACV customers and systematises cheaper implementations; Serval moved from FDEs for all customers to prioritising those with 1,000+ employees.
- Quota-style success metrics signal a sales role, not an FDE role; map a typical week to test whether the job is really engineering.
- Hiring traits: independent and outcome-oriented, grit for unglamorous work, high technical bar, constant building, curiosity about the customer's business. Palantir FDEs passed the same interview loops as software engineers, plus open-ended problems tied to real customer challenges (e.g. designing an insider-trading detection approach); Ironclad asked candidates to present a problem they had solved with technology.
- Onsite work can mean weeks on a customer's factory floor (a former Palantir FDE's example).
