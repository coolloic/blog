- **What:** preliminary research report (read via Fortune's coverage; the report itself is gated)
- **Publisher:** MIT NANDA initiative; lead author Aditya Challapally
- **Date:** July 2025 report; coverage 2025-08-18
- **Original:** [Fortune: MIT report, 95% of generative AI pilots failing](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)

## Summary

Surveys enterprise GenAI adoption and finds a sharp divide: a small minority of pilots produce rapid revenue or cost impact while most show none. Attributes the gap to tools that do not integrate with or learn from company workflows.

## Key claims

- Method (as reported by Fortune): about 150 leader interviews, a survey of 350 employees and analysis of 300 public AI deployments. The report's own methods note (January-June 2025) describes a review of 300+ public AI initiatives, structured interviews with representatives of 52 organisations and 153 senior-leader survey responses from industry conferences; the two descriptions do not match exactly.
- About 5% of pilots produce rapid revenue acceleration; the rest show little or no measurable P&L effect.
- Buying from or partnering with specialised vendors succeeds about 67% of the time; internal builds succeed about one third as often.
- The core barrier is a "learning gap": generic tools help individuals but stall in enterprise workflows because they do not retain feedback or adapt to context.
- Over half of GenAI budgets go to sales and marketing, but the largest returns come from back-office automation (cutting BPO, agency spend, operations).
- Empowering line managers, not only central AI labs, drives adoption; startups focused on one pain point see strong results.
- "Shadow AI" (unsanctioned personal ChatGPT use) is widespread.
- Caveat: small, anonymised sample in a preliminary report; the 95% headline is often quoted with more certainty than the data supports.
