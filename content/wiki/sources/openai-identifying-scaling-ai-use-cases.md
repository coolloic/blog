## Summary

A three-step method for enterprise use-case discovery: find where AI adds value, teach employees six recurring use-case types, then collect and prioritise ideas with an impact/effort quadrant. Drawn from OpenAI's customer deployments; written for ChatGPT Enterprise adoption but applicable to API projects.

## Key claims

- Based on 300 successful implementations, 4,000+ adoption surveys and 2M+ business users; the primitives come from 600+ customer use cases.
- Three principles: leadership should lead AI adoption; complex use cases often slow you down, so let employees find what works; hackathons, workshops and peer learning accelerate adoption.
- Look for opportunities in three areas: repetitive low-value tasks (ask people for their "anti-to-do list"), skill bottlenecks (waiting on another team's expertise), and navigating ambiguity (getting unstuck).
- Six primitives: content creation, research, coding, data analysis, ideation/strategy, automation.
- Impact/effort quadrant: **High ROI focus** (high value, low effort: start here), **Self-service** (low effort, individual use), **Scope and prioritize** (high value, high effort: often transformational, e.g. Indeed's job-match explanations, which took months of testing and iteration and lifted applications started by 20%), **Deprioritize** (high effort, low value, e.g. building something an existing tool already does).
- Re-score the matrix each quarter, since rising model capability can turn high-effort ideas into low-effort ones.
- Estée Lauder's repeatable process: cross-functional trio (business user, subject-matter expert, tech lead); design (two-page brief), prepare data, build and test, launch with user guide, pivot and scale from feedback.
- Next step is department workflow mapping: break multi-step workflows into tasks and map each to a primitive.
